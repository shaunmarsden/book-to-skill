# The Art of War — Index

**Author:** Sun Tzu. Ancient Chinese military text, public domain.

**What it argues:** Winning is decided mostly before any conflict starts, through preparation, positioning and information, not through force applied in the moment. A commander who understands the terrain, the enemy and their own side clearly enough can often win without a costly fight at all, and a commander who understands none of these is gambling regardless of how the fighting goes.

**Chapters structured in this example:**

1. [Laying Plans](chapters/01-laying-plans.md) — the five factors that decide an outcome before anything happens, and why comparing them honestly matters more than confidence
2. [Waging War](chapters/02-waging-war.md) — why a long, costly effort damages the side that wins it, not just the side that loses
3. [Attack by Stratagem](chapters/03-attack-by-stratagem.md) — winning without a direct, costly confrontation is a better outcome than winning through one
