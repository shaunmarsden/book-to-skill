# Chapter 1: Laying Plans

## Core Idea

An outcome is mostly decided before it begins, by comparing both sides honestly against the same five factors. Confidence is not one of the five; it is what people substitute for a factor they have not actually checked.

## The Five Factors

1. **Moral influence** — whether people genuinely support the effort, not just comply with it
2. **Weather** — timing and conditions outside anyone's control
3. **Terrain** — the ground the effort actually happens on, and who it favours
4. **Command** — whether the person leading is capable, not just senior
5. **Doctrine** — whether the organisation, roles and supply behind the effort are actually sound

## Why This Matters

Comparing both sides against these five factors, honestly, before committing, is what actually predicts the outcome. A side that skips the comparison because it feels confident is not more likely to win; it is just less prepared for the version of events where it turns out not to be.

## Where This Sits

This is the book's foundation: every later chapter assumes the reader will compare, not assume.
