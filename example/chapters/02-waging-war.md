# Chapter 2: Waging War

## Core Idea

A long, drawn-out effort damages the side that eventually wins it, not only the side that loses. Cost is not something that only matters if you lose; it is a cost either way, and a slow win can end up worse than a fast loss.

## Key Points

- Every extra period of sustained effort consumes resources at a compounding rate, not a flat one
- A force that wins slowly, after heavy sustained cost, often cannot actually capitalise on the win
- Speed and decisiveness matter because delay is expensive in itself, independent of the eventual result
- Dragging out an effort that could have been resolved faster risks exhausting the side that started it before any benefit is realised

## Where This Sits

Follows directly from Chapter 1: even a side that compared the five factors correctly and chose to act can still lose by acting too slowly once committed.
