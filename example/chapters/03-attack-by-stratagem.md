# Chapter 3: Attack by Stratagem

## Core Idea

Winning without a direct, costly confrontation is a better outcome than winning through one. The best result is the opponent's plan failing before it commits to a costly effort at all; a decisive confrontation is what happens once the better options have already been missed.

## The Ranked Options, Best to Worst

1. Disrupt the opponent's actual plan before they commit to it
2. Disrupt their alliances and support before it comes to a direct confrontation
3. Confront their forces directly, if it must come to that
4. Attack a fortified, prepared position, the worst option, since it costs the most for the least certain result

## Knowing When to Act

A side that understands both itself and its opponent clearly is rarely surprised by the outcome. A side that understands only itself, and not the other side, wins about half the time. A side that understands neither is gambling regardless of how any single confrontation goes.

## Where This Sits

Builds on Chapter 1's five factors and Chapter 2's cost argument: the actual goal is to win by making a costly confrontation unnecessary, not to win a confrontation as impressively as possible.
